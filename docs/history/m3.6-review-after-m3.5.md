everything built so far, a report to discuss, and the fixes the user approved. No new
feature beyond what the fixes needed (the operator's submission rates).

- **The review**: one reviewer, by hand (no agents), reading the approval service (submit,
  approve, amend, the three phases of `Service::sign`), the gateway (connection handler,
  exec, sign and git paths, the pre-receive hook server, admission), the signer queue, the
  dev keystore, `writ-sign`'s sign path, and the parts of `writ-core`, `writ-store` and
  `writ-crypto` they call; each finding checked against the roadmap (M4 to M10), the threat
  model, the open questions and the backlog. No finding let an agent sign, push or read
  more than its lease allows. One medium (sign request bodies), four low, one bug for M6, a
  few code quality items.
- **Decided with the user**: fix now everything that doesn't belong to a later milestone;
  S2 (allowed lines from a lease holder) and Q2 (work under the state lock) go to M4's
  checklist, Q1 (amendments and the certificate's expiry) to M6's, with its decision now;
  submission rates for all sources together, and both rates the operator's (`submissions`
  in `writd.yaml`), the defaults suiting one person's agents; the reader key's residual
  risk written in the threat model.

**Status:** done on 2026-11-01, in seven commits (one per step) and the review loop's.

## What was done

**Step 1, sign requests bounded in time and number** (S1, `fix:`): a sign request's body is
read within 60 s (`SIGN_BODY_DEADLINE`), and the daemon holds at most 16 sign requests at
once (`MAX_SIGN_REQUESTS`, the signer queue's size), a slot taken after `may_sign` and kept
until the answer; `read_within` shared with the API's bodies.

**Step 2, the rate window pruned** (S3, `fix:`): `Store::prune_ops`, called by the timer
sweep, deletes the operations an hour past the rate window (a clock set back by up to an hour
still counts them).

**Step 3, the reader key's copies wiped** (S4, `fix:`): rcgen's `zeroize` feature; its key
pair, and the copy for rustls on a failure, are wiped; what rustls and ring keep is a
residual risk row.

**Step 4, submission rates** (S5, `fix:`): `crates/writ-approval/src/service/submissions.rs`:
per source and for all sources together, a minute each, set by the operator (defaults 10 and
60, bounded 1-1,000 and 1-10,000, checked at load and at start); a burst's first refusal has
its line, the count of the others is written when the rate next lets one through or when its
minute ends (the sweep); a full table drops the quietest window, its count first.

**Step 5, a panicking signer** (Q4, `fix:`): the queue's worker catches a backend's panic,
answers `Unavailable`, logs one line (queue, key) and stops; the worker logs through its
creator's subscriber.

**Step 6, constructors** (Q3, Q5, `refactor:`): `Denial::new`, `Denial::internal`, the
service's `cert_event`; the hook's tests on the fake clock.

**Step 7, docs** (`docs:`): M4's checklist (fetch through `decide_fetch` and its counter,
connections bounded, revocation latency measured: S2, Q2), M6's (an amendment capped at the
lease's end: Q1), the decision log, this page.

## Decisions

- **Two submission rates, the operator's.** One rate per source alone let many sources (an
  allowed IPv6 /48 holds 65,536 /64s) make the daemon parse and log without bound; a rate
  for all sources bounds that. The defaults (10, 60) suit one person; a team raises them.
- **Every refusal over a rate is counted, even from a source that goes quiet.** The count was
  written only with the source's next accepted submission, and a full table cleared every
  pending count (hard rule 9). Now a window's end writes its count, and a full table drops
  one window, its count first, instead of starting over.
- **A sign slot is refused at once, never queued**, like the signer's `Busy`: nothing waits
  behind a stuck device or a slow peer, and the slot is held until the answer so the body's
  memory stays bounded.
- **A panicking backend is never called again**: it may be in any state; signing stays
  unavailable until a restart, said once in the log.
- **An amendment changes scope, not time** (built in M6): the certificate keeps the lease's
  end.
- **No pruning on every request**: only the timer sweep deletes old operations (a write each
  time otherwise), with an hour's margin against a clock set back.

## The review loop

After the seven steps, the code they changed was reviewed again for security, by hand, until
a pass found nothing more.

## The security checklist, for M3.6

1. **Inputs**: the sign request body gains a time bound and a daemon-wide count; submissions
   a bound for all sources together; both in the inventory, with tests on both sides.
2. **Authority**: not touched.
3. **Fail closed**: no sign slot or a slow body refuses; a panicking backend signs nothing
   more; pruning never removes a row a window counts; rates out of bounds stop the daemon at
   start.
4. **Audit**: every submission refused over a rate is counted in the log (a quiet source and
   a full table included); the new sign refusals are logged like any gateway refusal.
5. **Revocation**: not touched.
6. **Secrets**: rcgen's copy of the reader key is wiped; what rustls and ring keep is
   written down.
7. **Display**: not touched.
8. **Dependencies**: rcgen's `zeroize` feature; `tracing` in `writ-signer`; no new crate.
9. **Guarantees**: not touched (availability and audit completeness; "everything logged"
   gains the counted submissions).
10. **Threat model**: the reader key's residual risk (approved), the submissions row updated.
