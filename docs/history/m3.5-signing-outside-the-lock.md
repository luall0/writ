# M3.5 — Signing outside the lock

From the [review after M3](m3-signed-commits.md#the-review-after-m3) (finding F5).
`Service::sign` called the `Signer` while holding the service's state lock: harmless with the
software backend, but a Secure Enclave, a YubiKey or a hung device would have held up every
approval, wait, refusal and **revocation** while it signed. It had to land before M4 (which
adds network I/O and the upstream credential through the `Signer`) and before M7 (the M7 and
M7.5 graft tests allow no change to `writ-approval` beyond bug fixes).

Planned:

- **`Service::sign` in three phases.** Under the lock: the lease, `decide_sign`, `consume`;
  without it: the signature, through the queue, with a deadline; under the lock again: the
  certificate not on the deny list, the lease still active and not expired, then `signed_id`,
  the audit line, `ledger_append`, and only then does the signature leave. A failed or
  discarded signature keeps its slot.
- **`QueuedSigner`**, a `Signer` decorator: one worker thread owns the backend, a bounded
  queue (`Busy`), a deadline per job (`Timeout`), `list_keys` from a cache; one queue per use.
- Docs: decision log, `signer.md`, `policy-and-state.md`, flows § 2, the threat model.

**Status:** done on 2026-11-01, in two `feat:` steps, one `test:` step for the property test,
and a `test:` + `docs:` pair for the integration scenario and this page.

## What was done

**Step 1, the `QueuedSigner`** (`writ_signer::queue`). A decorator, itself a `Signer`, so the
core doesn't change: one worker thread owns the backend and signs one job at a time; a
bounded queue answers `SignerError::Busy` at once when full; each job has a deadline
(`SignerError::Timeout`); a job whose caller gave up (its `Pending` dropped, or its wait timed
out) is skipped, a late result thrown away; `list_keys` and `capabilities` answer from a cache
filled at startup. `SignerError` gained `Busy`, `Timeout` and `Unavailable`; a shared signer
(`Arc<S>`) is a `Signer`. `writd` puts the agent keys behind their own queue (16 jobs, 10 s).
Test support: `testing::GatedSigner`, which holds each signature until the test releases it
(one call, or all), says when one is waiting (`wait_entered`, an `on_enter` callback), and
whose `close()` returns a guard that opens the gate on drop; `FailingSigner::fail_with` for
any error. The contract's decorator section (`queued_signer_contract`), run by the software
backend.

**Step 2, three phases** (`writ-approval`, `service/sign.rs`). `reserve` (under the lock:
the lease, `decide_sign`, `consume`; denials logged, a violation's certificate barred before
the lock is released), the signature, `settle` (under the lock: the lease's terms unchanged,
active, not past `expires_at`, the certificate not on the deny list; then the signed id, the
audit line and the ledger; otherwise the signature is discarded and the denial logged, "…
(while the commit was being signed); the signature was discarded"). A lease with a
signature in flight isn't marked exhausted by the sweep until the signature is settled
(`InFlightSignatures`, released by an `InFlight` guard even if the backend panics). The
signer's `Busy` and `Timeout` reach the agent with their own reason.

**Step 3, the property test** (`crates/writ-approval/tests/sign_phases.rs`): concurrent
requests driven a phase at a time, the clock moving, a revocation at any point. Run with the
fixed seed and a random one by `make test`, and by `make test-props`.

**Step 4, the real-git scenario** (`a_revocation_completes_while_a_commit_is_being_signed`),
then this page.

## Decisions

- **One queue per use**, not per device (decision log): the TLS key (M5) and the upstream
  credential (M4) get their own queue when they move behind the `Signer`, so a device stuck
  on commit signing can't take the HTTPS approval API (and revocation over HTTPS) with it.
- **The backend stays `Send + Sync`**: the trait's bound is kept; a device that isn't would
  get its own adapter. The worker still serializes calls, for devices that take one session
  at a time.
- **A slot taken is never given back** (as planned): a signature that fails (signer, audit
  log, ledger) or is discarded keeps its count and rate slot.
- **A lease with a signature in flight isn't marked exhausted** until that signature is
  settled. Found while designing step 2: the slot taken in the first phase makes the lease
  look used up, and the sweep (any request's, or the daemon's timer) would have marked it so
  and barred its certificate, so the last phase would always have discarded the last
  signature. The rule only delays the state change; the counter still refuses past the limit.
- **The last phase compares the lease's whole terms**, not only its state: an amendment
  approved while the commit was being signed discards the signature (`not_active`, "its terms
  changed while the commit was being signed"). The decision was made on the old terms.
- **The skip is by abandonment, not by the clock**: the worker skips a job whose caller gave
  up, so a test can hold the backend and know which jobs reach it without racing a deadline.

## Findings

- **The exhaustion trap** above, before any code: without the in-flight rule, a lease's last
  signature would always have been discarded.
- **A failing assertion while a signature waited at the gate hung the test** (the scope
  waited on a thread held forever) instead of failing it, seen while checking that the step-2
  tests fail without the fix. `GatedSigner::close` now returns a guard that opens the gate on
  drop.
- **The property test missed the exhaustion trap at first**: it only bounded signatures from
  above, and "fewer" passed. It now also checks that no signature is discarded without a
  reason, and fails with the in-flight rule removed.
- **A revoked session is dropped before its signature comes back**: through the gateway, a
  revocation drops the lease's sessions at once, so the agent reads "writ: the lease ended"
  without waiting for the device; the call left in the daemon then refuses the signature in
  its last phase (`not_active`), and nothing is recorded.

## The security checklist, for M3.5

1. **Inputs**: sign requests are also bounded by the agent keys' queue (16 waiting, 10 s each);
   the slot is taken before the signer is asked, so a flood of requests is held by the count
   and the rate first.
2. **Authority**: nothing new grants.
3. **Fail closed**: a full queue, a timeout, a dead worker, an unknown key, a clock or lock
   failure in the last phase, a lease that ended or changed: each denies, the signature
   discarded. A poisoned lock in the in-flight guard is recovered (releasing it is a reduction
   of privilege).
4. **Audit**: one `sign` line per call; the allowed line only after the last check; a
   discarded signature is a denial with its reason.
5. **Revocation**: a revocation, expiry, violation or amendment while the commit is being
   signed discards the signature; the last phase checks the deny list, not only the store.
   Nothing under the state lock waits for a device, so a revocation never waits behind one.
6. **Secrets**: signer errors reach the agent by kind only (busy, late, failed); no key in
   `QueuedSigner`'s `Debug`.
7. **Display**: not touched.
8. **Dependencies**: none added (`proptest`, already decided, is a dev-dependency of
   `writ-approval` now).
9. **Guarantees**: "leases bounded in time, count and rate; revocable instantly" gains six
   tests (five in-flight cases and the property test).
10. **Threat model**: the residual risk "the signer runs under the state lock" is gone;
    "a failed signature still uses its slot" replaces it (planned in the roadmap). No
    guarantee weakened.

## Acceptance

- [x] With a signer that blocks until the test releases it (a signal, never a `sleep`): a
      revocation completes while a signature is in flight, and that signature is then refused
      with its exact code; approvals, `writ wait` and `writ ls` answer meanwhile.
  *`a_revocation_completes_while_a_signature_is_in_flight` (the service, a `GatedSigner`):
  while the signature waits, a ticket is submitted, approved and waited for, the listing
  answers, the revocation completes, each on its own thread with a bound that fails the test
  instead of hanging; released, the signature is refused `not_active`, one `sign` line, its
  slot used, nothing in the ledger. Also an expiry (`expired`), an amendment, and the last
  slot (`a_lease_that_expires_while_signing_discards_the_signature`,
  `a_lease_amended_while_signing_discards_the_signature`,
  `the_last_signature_in_flight_is_not_refused_as_used_up`). Through real `git`, `ssh` and
  `writ-sign`: `a_revocation_completes_while_a_commit_is_being_signed` (the agent reads "the
  lease ended" at once; the held call ends with its `not_active` line; no commit, no ledger
  entry). Seen to fail with the last phase's lease check removed.*
- [x] A violation puts the certificate on the deny list before the lock is released: a second
      request of the lease waiting on the lock is refused (`not_active`).
  *With the signer outside the lock, the window this guarded moved to the last phase: a
  signature in flight when another request violates. `a_violation_while_a_signature_is_in_flight_discards_it`:
  the violation's revocation fails to record (`StoreBreaker`), the store still says active,
  and the signature in flight is refused `not_active` through the deny list alone. A request
  after the violation: `a_violation_bars_the_certificate_even_when_its_revocation_fails`
  (since the review).*
- [x] The `Signer` contract suite gains the decorator section: a full queue refuses at once
      with `Busy`; a backend that blocks returns `Timeout`; `list_keys` answers while the
      backend is stuck. The software backend runs it behind a `QueuedSigner`.
  *`writ_signer::contract::queued_signer_contract`, run by
  `the_software_backend_passes_the_contract_behind_a_queue` (Ed25519; P-256 with two keys):
  the whole contract through the queue; with the backend held, keys and capabilities
  answer, a full queue refuses with `Busy` (through `submit` and the trait), the held job and
  the one queued behind it return `Timeout` with their key, the abandoned job is never
  signed, the next one signs and verifies. The service maps `Busy`, `Timeout` and
  `Unavailable` to their reasons (`a_busy_or_late_signer_is_refused_with_its_reason`).*
- [x] Count and rate hold under concurrent requests (a property test over interleavings of
      the phases): never more signatures than `max_signatures`, never more than the rate.
  *`count_and_rate_hold_whatever_the_phases_interleaving` (256 cases per pass): also none
  after a revocation, none discarded without a reason, each logged and in the ledger, the
  lease's end state. Seen to fail with `consume` taken out of the first phase (the shrunk
  case kept in the regressions file) and with the in-flight rule removed.*
- [x] Every M3 acceptance scenario still passes unchanged.
  *`make ci`: every scenario in `signed_commits.rs` and `writ_init.rs`, unchanged. One
  service test changed on purpose: `a_failing_signer_or_audit_log_signs_nothing` became
  `…_and_keeps_the_slot` (the slot is now taken first and never given back).*
